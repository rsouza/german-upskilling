# Articles & Cases — Artikel und Fälle

The four cases are the backbone of German. Once you internalize this table, everything else clicks.

---

## The Four Cases — What They Do

| Case | Role | Trigger question |
|------|------|-----------------|
| **Nominativ** | subject of the sentence | *Wer/Was macht etwas?* |
| **Akkusativ** | direct object (receives the action) | *Wen/Was?* |
| **Dativ** | indirect object; after certain prepositions | *Wem? / Mit wem?* |
| **Genitiv** | possession | *Wessen?* |

---

## Definite Articles — *der, die, das* (the)

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|-|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Nom** | **der** | **die** | **das** | **die** |
| **Akk** | **den** | **die** | **das** | **die** |
| **Dat** | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den** (+n to noun) |
| **Gen** | **des** (+s to noun) | **der** | **des** (+s to noun) | **der** |

**Memory trick:** Nominative → Accusative: only *der* (masc.) changes to *den*.

---

## Indefinite Articles — *ein, eine* (a / an)

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|-|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Nom** | **ein** | **eine** | **ein** | — |
| **Akk** | **einen** | **eine** | **ein** | — |
| **Dat** | **einem** | **einer** | **einem** | — |
| **Gen** | **eines** | **einer** | **eines** | — |

> Plural of *ein* doesn't exist — use no article (*Ich kaufe Äpfel.*)  
> or *manche/einige* (some).

---

## Negative Article — *kein* (no / not a)

Follows the same pattern as *ein*, plus plural:

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|-|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Nom** | **kein** | **keine** | **kein** | **keine** |
| **Akk** | **keinen** | **keine** | **kein** | **keine** |
| **Dat** | **keinem** | **keiner** | **keinem** | **keinen** |
| **Gen** | **keines** | **keiner** | **keines** | **keiner** |

> All possessives (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr) follow this exact pattern.

## Case in Practice

**Nominative** — the subject:
- ***Der Chef** kommt gleich.* — The boss is coming soon.
- ***Eine Kollegin** hat angerufen.* — A colleague called.

**Accusative** — direct object:
- *Ich brauche **den Bericht**.* — I need the report.
- *Haben Sie **einen Moment**?* — Do you have a moment?

**Dative** — indirect object or after dative prepositions (mit, bei, von, zu, aus, seit, nach, gegenüber):
- *Ich gebe **dem Kollegen** die Unterlagen.* — I'm giving the colleague the documents.
- *Ich fahre **mit dem** Zug.* — I'm going by train.
- *Ich wohne **bei einer** Freundin.* — I live at a friend's (female).

**Genitive** — possession (mostly written; spoken often replaced by *von + Dat*):
- *Das ist das Büro **des Direktors**.* — That's the director's office.
- *Das ist das Büro **von dem** Direktor.* (spoken equivalent)

## Gender Quick Guide

German nouns have grammatical gender. You learn it with the noun.  
Some patterns help:

| Pattern | Gender | Examples |
|---------|--------|---------|
| *-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion* | always feminine (die) | *die Sitzung, die Freiheit, die Nation* |
| *-er, -ismus, -or* (people/agents) | usually masculine (der) | *der Lehrer, der Direktor* |
| *-chen, -lein* (diminutives) | always neuter (das) | *das Mädchen, das Büchlein* |
| *-ment, -um* | usually neuter (das) | *das Dokument, das Zentrum* |
| Infinitives used as nouns | always neuter (das) | *das Essen, das Arbeiten* |

> When in doubt: **die** is the most common gender in German.
