# Word Order — Satzstellung

German word order follows clear rules. Once you internalise them, complex sentences become possible.

---

## Rule 1: The Verb Is Always in Position 2 (Main Clause)

The **finite verb** (the conjugated verb) always occupies position 2 in a main clause.

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Rest |
|-----------|-----------|------|
| Ich | **arbeite** | bei RBI. |
| Morgen | **habe** | ich ein Meeting. |
| Das | **ist** | sehr interessant. |
| Leider | **kann** | ich nicht kommen. |

> Position 1 can be the subject, a time word, an adverb — anything. If it's not the subject, the subject jumps after the verb (inversion).

---

## Rule 2: Time — Manner — Place (TeKaMoLo)

When describing a situation, the preferred order is:
**Te**mporal (when) → **Ka**usal (why) → **Mo**dal (how) → **Lo**kal (where)

- *Ich fahre **morgen** (Te) **mit dem Zug** (Mo) **nach Wien** (Lo).* — I'm going to Vienna by train tomorrow.
- *Er kommt **wegen des Meetings** (Ka) **nicht** (Mo) **ins Büro** (Lo).* — He's not coming to the office because of the meeting.

---

## Rule 3: Separable Verbs — Prefix to End

- *Ich **rufe** Sie morgen **an**.* — I'll call you tomorrow.
- *Das Meeting **fängt** um 10 Uhr **an**.* — The meeting starts at 10.
- *Wann **kommen** Sie **zurück**?* — When are you coming back?

---

## Rule 4: Modal Verbs — Infinitive to End

- *Ich **muss** den Bericht **schreiben**.* — I have to write the report.
- *Können Sie das bitte **wiederholen**?* — Could you please repeat that?
- *Wir **sollten** das **besprechen**.* — We should discuss that.

---

## Rule 5: Perfekt — Partizip II to End

- *Ich **habe** das Dokument **geschickt**.* — I sent the document.
- *Wir **haben** das Problem **gelöst**.* — We solved the problem.
- *Ich **bin** gestern zu spät **gekommen**.* — I came too late yesterday.

---

## Rule 6: Subordinate Clauses — Verb to End

After subordinating conjunctions, the **conjugated verb goes to the very end**.

**Common subordinating conjunctions:**

| Conjunction | English |
|------------|---------|
| **dass** | that |
| **weil** | because |
| **wenn** | when / if |
| **ob** | whether / if |
| **obwohl** | although |
| **damit** | so that |
| **bevor** | before |
| **nachdem** | after |
| **während** | while |
| **bis** | until |
| **seitdem** | since |

**Examples:**
- *Ich denke, dass das Meeting zu lang **ist**.* — I think that the meeting is too long.
- *Ich kann nicht kommen, weil ich einen anderen Termin **habe**.* — I can't come because I have another appointment.
- *Wenn Sie Fragen **haben**, können Sie mich anrufen.* — If you have questions, you can call me.
- *Ich weiß nicht, ob er **kommt**.* — I don't know whether he's coming.

**Perfekt in subordinate clause:**
- *Er sagt, dass er das schon **gemacht hat**.* — He says that he already did that.
> (In subordinate clauses: Partizip II comes before haben/sein at the very end)

**Modal in subordinate clause:**
- *Ich glaube, dass wir das **besprechen müssen**.* — I think we need to discuss that.
> (Infinitive before modal at the end)

---

## Rule 7: Relative Clauses — Verb to End

Relative clauses are subordinate clauses — verb goes to end.

- *Der Kollege, **der** immer zu spät **kommt**, heißt Klaus.* — The colleague who always comes late is called Klaus.
- *Das Projekt, **an dem** wir **arbeiten**, ist sehr wichtig.* — The project we're working on is very important.

## Questions

**Yes/No questions:** verb comes first
- ***Sprechen** Sie Deutsch?* — Do you speak German?
- ***Haben** Sie einen Moment?* — Do you have a moment?

**W-questions:** question word → verb → subject → rest
- ***Wann** **beginnt** das Meeting?* — When does the meeting start?
- ***Wo** **ist** das Büro?* — Where is the office?
- ***Warum** **können** Sie nicht **kommen**?* — Why can't you come?

---

## Negation

**nicht** (negates verbs, adjectives, specific elements):
- *Ich komme **nicht**.* — I'm not coming.
- *Das ist **nicht** richtig.* — That's not right.
- *Ich verstehe das **nicht**.* — I don't understand that.

**kein-** (negates nouns with articles):
- *Ich habe **keine** Zeit.* — I have no time.
- *Ich spreche **kein** Deutsch.* — I don't speak German.
- *Es gibt **kein** Problem.* — There's no problem.
