# Die Gedanken sind frei
*Traditional German folk song | Public domain | Level: A2*

One of the most beloved German folk songs, dating to the early 19th century. It became a symbol of resistance and inner freedom. You'll find it referenced throughout German-speaking culture.

---

## Lyrics & Translation

| German | English |
|--------|---------|
| **Strophe 1** | **Verse 1** |
| Die Gedanken sind frei, | Thoughts are free, |
| wer kann sie erraten? | who can guess them? |
| Sie fliehen vorbei | They fly past |
| wie nächtliche Schatten. | like shadows of the night. |
| Kein Mensch kann sie wissen, | No person can know them, |
| kein Jäger erschießen | no hunter can shoot them |
| mit Pulver und Blei: | with powder and lead: |
| Die Gedanken sind frei! | Thoughts are free! |
| **Strophe 2** | **Verse 2** |
| Ich denke, was ich will | I think what I want |
| und was mich beglücket, | and what makes me happy, |
| doch alles in der Still | yet all in silence |
| und wie es sich schicket. | and as is proper. |
| Mein Wunsch und Begehren | My wish and desire |
| kann niemand verwehren, | no one can deny, |
| es bleibet dabei: | it remains so: |
| Die Gedanken sind frei! | Thoughts are free! |
| **Strophe 3** | **Verse 3** |
| Und sperrt man mich ein | And if I am locked up |
| im finsteren Kerker, | in a dark dungeon, |
| das alles sind rein | all of that is purely |

| denn meine Gedanken | for my thoughts |

| und Mauern entzwei: | and walls asunder: |
| Die Gedanken sind frei! | Thoughts are free! |

## Grammar Highlights

**Kein Mensch kann sie wissen** — *niemand / kein Mensch* = no one (interchangeable)

**Und sperrt man mich ein** — *man* = one / you / people (impersonal subject, very common in German)
- *Man kann das nicht wissen.* = One cannot know that. / You can't know that.
- *Man spricht hier Deutsch.* = German is spoken here.

**zerreißen die Schranken** — *zer-* as a destructive prefix: breaks, tears, destroys
- *zerstören* (to destroy), *zerbrechen* (to break), *zerschneiden* (to cut up)

## Cultural Note

This song dates to at least 1780 and was used as a protest song during times of censorship and political repression — in 1848, under Nazism, and in the GDR. Sophie Scholl (the White Rose resistance member) was reportedly found with this song before her execution in 1943. It is still sung at German and Austrian folk festivals today.

**Listen:** Search "Die Gedanken sind frei" on YouTube — dozens of versions exist, from folk to orchestral.
